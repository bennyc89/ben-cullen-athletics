For Parents & Players
Simple ideas to help young players build good habits early.
Why We Warm Up
Warming up gets the body ready to move — like waking muscles up before they go to work. A good warm-up means better movement and fewer knocks and niggles.
Growing & Learning Together
At this age, it's completely normal for coordination to look a little wobbly some weeks and sharp the next. That's growth and learning happening — not a step backwards.
Listening to Your Body
We encourage every young athlete to tell a coach or parent if something feels sore or tired. Learning to listen to their body early is a skill for life, not just tennis.
Nutrition Basics
Simple ideas for parents — general guidance only.
Food as Fuel
Explaining food simply as what gives the body energy to play helps young athletes build a healthy, positive relationship with eating.
Hydration
Regular water through the day, and especially around training, helps young athletes feel their best.
Balanced Plates
A mix of different food groups supports growing, active bodies. This age should always be about eating enough, never about restriction.
A Note for Parents
This is general guidance only. For any specific dietary questions, please speak with your GP or a qualified paediatric nutritionist.

Knowledge Hub
Understanding the why behind the programme.
Why We Test First
Baseline testing isn't about pass or fail — it just gives us a real starting point, so progress over the term is easy to see and prove.
Growth Spurts Happen
Around this age, growth spurts can make coordination feel temporarily off. This is completely normal and passes — consistent practice through it is what matters, not perfection.
Warm-Up Isn't Optional
A proper warm-up before every session is part of the training, not a formality before it. It's one of the simplest ways to train harder and stay injury-free.
Rest Days Count Too
The body adapts and gets stronger during rest, not just during training. Skipping recovery time can undo the benefit of a hard session.
Nutrition Basics
General guidance to support training and growth.
Eating Before Training
A simple snack an hour or so before training — like toast or a banana — helps energy levels during the session.
Hydration Habits
Building the habit of drinking water regularly, not just when thirsty, supports both training and concentration at school.
Balanced, Not Restricted
Growing athletes need enough food to support both training and natural growth. This is never about eating less.
Any Questions?
For specific dietary needs or concerns, please speak with your GP or a qualified nutritionist — this section is general guidance only.

Knowledge Hub
Deeper context as training intensity increases.
Understanding Training Load
As sessions get more demanding, we structure load carefully across the block so the body adapts without being overwhelmed. If something feels consistently too hard, tell your coach.
Sleep & Recovery Matter
Teenage athletes in heavier training need good sleep to recover and grow. It's as much a part of performance as the sessions themselves.
Building Strength Safely
Strength work at this age is about technique first, load second. Getting the movement right matters far more than how much weight is used.
The Mental Side
Confidence and composure under pressure are trainable, just like speed or strength. It's normal for this to take time to develop.
Nutrition Basics
Supporting training demands and continued growth.
Fuelling Growth & Training
This age combines natural growth with increasing training demands — both need adequate fuel. The focus should always be on eating enough, not less.
Hydration for Longer Sessions
As session length and intensity increase, staying consistently hydrated through the day becomes more important, not just during training.
After-Training Recovery
Simple recovery snacks with a mix of carbohydrate and protein — like a sandwich or yoghurt with fruit — support recovery between sessions.
